What we mean by a self-hosted cloud.

The same services you already lean on. Files, photos, media, passwords. You manage the server. For this stack, most of it runs on hardware at home.
// your hardware
Runs at home.
A mini PC, a NAS, an old desktop. Your data lives on disks you can physically touch.
// open source
Open source apps.
Nextcloud, Immich, and Jellyfin. You control the installation and when it changes.
// still a cloud
Reachable anywhere.
Your phone, your laptop, a coffee shop. Securely, without forwarding ports on your router.

Why self-host?
The pitch, the subscription treadmill, and the honest trade-offs.
the pitch, part one

Who controls
your access?

  • Cloud services store your files, photos, and documents on their infrastructure.
  • TOS change. Accounts get banned. Companies shut down.
  • Self-hosting gives you control over the server, its data, and its backups.

why people switch

The subscription treadmill

Files, photos, media. Every service becomes another monthly bill.

The useful question is which services you'd actually want to run yourself.

The costs move

Hardware and storage up front.

Power, backups, and your time after that.

What you save depends on what you replace.

what ownership involves

Living with the stack

  • Maintenance becomes a habit.
    Read update notes, check backups, and practice a restore.
  • Your household depends on it.
    When something breaks, you're the person fixing it.
  • Some cloud services can stay.
    Choose what fits your time and the people using it.

Nextcloud   files and tasks

My files and tasks,
in one place

  • I use Nextcloud for general file storage and organizing tasks.
  • Files are available in the browser and through desktop or mobile clients.
  • Nextcloud AIO packages the services together. I run it in a Proxmox VM.
  • I still manage updates, storage, and backups.
Immich   replaces Google Photos

My photo library
at home

  • My phone uploads photos and videos to Immich.
  • I can find them again by person, place, or search.
  • The server needs its own backup, including the photos and database.
  • First stable release: October 1, 2025.
Jellyfin   your own streaming service

My media library,
ready to watch

  • Browse my movies and shows, then pick up from Continue Watching.
  • Jellyfin organizes and streams the media I supply.
  • Free and open source, including hardware transcoding.
  • Playback depends on the client. Some files need the server to convert them.
Vaultwarden   replaces 1Password / LastPass

Your passwords, self-hosted.

  • Lightweight Bitwarden-compatible server, written in Rust.
  • Works with the official Bitwarden clients. Browser, mobile, desktop.
  • End-to-end encrypted. Sync across devices without trusting a cloud.
  • Replaces 1Password, LastPass, hosted Bitwarden.
Home Assistant   your smart home hub

A home that works
locally

  • Local control of lights, sensors, automations, dashboards.
  • Local devices and integrations can keep working without the cloud.
  • Works with thousands of devices and integrations.
  • Some integrations still depend on a vendor cloud. Check before buying devices.
Frigate

Camera recordings
kept at home

  • Local recording and object detection for compatible cameras.
  • Choose a detector for your hardware: Intel OpenVINO, a GPU, or Coral.
  • Detection events can trigger Home Assistant automations.
NGINX Proxy Manager   clean local access

Local names
and HTTPS

  • Clean local domain names and HTTPS for internal services.
  • Local DNS points at NPM. It forwards each name to the right service.
  • In my setup, NPM serves the home network. It is not forwarded through my router.
  • NetBird handles remote access separately.

reverse proxy

Sharing a service without a client

  • For a service someone needs to open in a regular browser.
  • The public hostname reaches a proxy on the VPS, which connects to the home service over NetBird.
  • Configure access protection for the service you publish.
  • This is public sharing. My private services remain on the client connection.

Backups are non-negotiable.
Because drives fail. It's not if. It's when.
what matters to me

What I actually
back up

  • Photos and documents.
    The personal stuff I can't replace.
  • App data and configurations.
    The work that makes these services mine.
  • VMs and containers.
    A way to bring the services back.

My movie and TV library doesn't need the same protection.

the rule

The 3-2-1 rule

3
// COPIES
Three copies of your data.
Your working data plus two separate backups.
live · backup · backup
2
// MEDIA TYPES
On two different media.
Use different storage media to reduce shared failure risks.
SSD + hard drive, or disk + tape
1
// OFFSITE
One copy offsite.
Fire, theft, flood. If the building is gone, the backup in it is gone too.
friend's house · object storage · vps
local recovery + an offsite copy

My backup setup

  1. My home servers

    Proxmox VMs and containers, plus selected files from my NAS.

  2. Proxmox Backup Server (PBS)

    A separate box at home for faster local restores.

  3. S3-compatible storage offsite

    A scheduled copy after the local backups finish.

The first offsite upload was still running after a couple of days.
