The Agent Arena Hackathon · San Francisco

~45 sec. Here's the picture. Each machine runs the NetBird agent, and you sign in through your identity provider: Okta, Google, whatever your team already uses. Both peers keep a control channel open to the control plane, which is our cloud or your own server. Management holds the network map and your policies, and your private keys never leave your machine. When Peer A wants to talk to Peer B, they use STUN to find their public addresses, trade connection details through Signal, and then build a WireGuard tunnel straight to each other. Your traffic doesn't go through us. And if a firewall blocks the direct path, it falls back to the relay, still encrypted end to end.

~45 sec. That's the connect half. Now the expose half. Everything so far is private, but at some point this weekend someone outside your network has to see your work: a judge, a webhook, a friend's browser. That's the reverse proxy. A request comes in over HTTPS, the NetBird proxy handles the certificate and the login, then sends it down a WireGuard tunnel to your service. Your service still has no public IP and no open ports. Put SSO, a password, a PIN or an API key in front of it, filter by IP or country, and use your own domain if you want. It handles HTTP, plus raw TCP, UDP and TLS.

~40 sec. The dashboard is great for things you want up all weekend. For everything temporary, there's netbird expose. Give it a port, and you get back a public HTTPS link.
