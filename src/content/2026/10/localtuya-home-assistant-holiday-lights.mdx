It was December, so naturally it was time for Christmas lights. A company called XMCOSY sent over a pile of their smart lights, and my first instinct was to say no. Then I did a little research and found out there was a decent chance I could get them working in Home Assistant without going through the cloud, which is a hard requirement for my setup. So I said yes, and then I spent a very late night figuring it out.

The end result: static colors, brightness, color temperature, and the app's animated scenes, plus my own custom scenes, all running from Home Assistant over my local network. In this guide I'll go over the lights and their app, why Tuya devices need a cloud account at all, and the full LocalTuya setup, including how to pull scene strings out of the Tuya developer platform.

XMCOSY sent me the lights in this article for free. They didn't pay me for the video, but there's still bias there, so keep that in mind.

The video was published in December 2024. The written steps have been updated for [hass-localtuya 2026.7.0](https://github.com/xZetsubou/hass-localtuya/releases/tag/2026.7.0), released July 17, 2026, and for the current HACS and Tuya Developer Platform docs. Since recording, the fork I used has been renamed from `xZetsubou/localtuya` to `xZetsubou/hass-localtuya`, it now requires Home Assistant 2025.1.0 or newer, and Tuya's current guide lists a different set of API services for new projects than the wizard showed me. My hands-on impressions and screenshots come from the original recording, and I've called out the changes where they matter.

## The XMCOSY Lights

The star of the show is the **Smart RGBCW Acrylic String Lights**. They have that faceted, kind of Christmasy look, and I got two packs to run across the front of my house. The cords are thick, the acrylic feels nice, and as far as I can tell they're decent quality. I believe you can daisy chain them, too.

They also sent a pack of globe-style string lights and a two-pack of smart wall lights. I didn't set the wall lights up for this one because I didn't feel like doing electrical work that day.

![The box for the XMCOSY Smart RGBCW Acrylic String Lights, a 30-bulb, 96-foot set with faceted acrylic bulbs](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/acrylic-string-lights-box.jpg)

All of these connect over **2.4 GHz Wi-Fi**. Mine live on my IoT network, and I gave each one a reserved IP address so I could keep track of them. In the Tuya app and in Home Assistant I named them by bulb style and count, like `XMcosy Acrylic (24)`, `XMcosy Acrylic (30)`, and `XMcosy Rounded (30)`.

## The XMCOSY App

It wouldn't be fair to skip straight to the nerdy stuff, so here's the normal way to use them. Pairing in the [XMCOSY](https://xmcosy.com) app is simple:

1. Plug in the lights and tap **Add Device**.
2. Reset the lights by holding the button on the controller box until the bulbs start flashing.
3. Pick your 2.4 GHz network, enter the password, and let it connect. The lights go solid white when they're done.

The app is honestly pretty cool. You can tap individual bulbs and set each one to its own color, so you can build a pattern by hand. There are DIY scenes where you pick an effect (chase, gradient, and a bunch I couldn't identify), a speed, and a set of colors. There are also lots of premade scenes sorted into categories like scenery, life, festival, and mood, with things like Christmas, Thanksgiving, and a Halloween one that fades between Halloween colors. The Christmas preset isn't what I'd use, but to each their own.

There's a music mode that uses your phone's microphone, which works okay. It would be a lot better if you could pipe audio in directly instead of using the mic. And there are timers and all the other basic stuff.

If you don't want to deal with Home Assistant, the app is a perfectly good way to run these. I just don't want to depend on the cloud or an app that isn't Home Assistant.

## Why Tuya, and Why LocalTuya

The app is XMCOSY-branded, but the technology underneath is [Tuya](https://www.tuya.com). Lots of brands license Tuya's platform for their devices, and those devices also work in Tuya's own **Tuya Smart** and **Smart Life** apps. That's the important part, because adding your devices to the Tuya app gives you access to Tuya's developer platform and its API.

The reason we need the API is the **local key**. These devices can be controlled locally, but only if you have each device's ID and local key, and Tuya only hands those out through its cloud. Once you have them, you technically don't need the cloud anymore.

There are a few ways to get Tuya devices into Home Assistant:

| Option | How it talks to the lights | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Official Tuya integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/tuya/) | Cloud (Cloud Push) | Built in. Sign in with a user code from the Smart Life or Tuya Smart app. No developer account needed. |
| [rospogrigio/localtuya](https://github.com/rospogrigio/localtuya) | Local | The original LocalTuya. Listed in HACS by default. Its README lists Tuya protocols 3.1 to 3.4. |
| [xZetsubou/hass-localtuya](https://github.com/xZetsubou/hass-localtuya) | Local | A fork of LocalTuya. Supports protocols 3.1 to 3.5, adds cloud-assisted auto-configuration, and is the one I used. |

If you don't care about the cloud, the official integration is the easy path. That's not what I'm here for.

I found a couple of guides for these exact lights, including [one I was really hoping would work](https://thehelpfulidiot.com/integrating-xmcosy-outdoor-string-lights-with-home-assistant-locally). It's a good read and might work if you have older lights, but it didn't work with mine. The original LocalTuya didn't either. What finally worked was **xZetsubou's fork**, which had the newer protocol support these lights needed. My lights reported protocol `3.5`, which the fork supports and the original's README doesn't list. It took me a while to figure that out, and if I'd known it from the start this video would have been out a lot sooner.

As of October 2026, both projects are still alive, but at very different speeds. The original's last release was [v5.2.5](https://github.com/rospogrigio/localtuya/releases/tag/v5.2.5) in January 2026, a small config flow fix, and it has more than 1,400 open issues. The fork shipped [2026.7.0](https://github.com/xZetsubou/hass-localtuya/releases/tag/2026.7.0) in July, which includes a fix for protocol 3.5 reconnects, and it was still getting commits in late September. This guide is written for the fork.

<Note>
Both projects install as the same `localtuya` integration, so you can't run them side by side. If you already have the original installed from HACS, remove it before installing the fork.
</Note>

## What You'll Need

- **Home Assistant with HACS.** If you're starting from scratch, my [Home Assistant setup guide](/home-assistant-smart-home-setup) covers getting HA running. HACS needs Home Assistant 2024.4.1 or newer, and the fork currently requires Home Assistant 2025.1.0 or newer.
- **A GitHub account**, which HACS uses to sign in during its setup.
- **The Tuya Smart or Smart Life app** with your lights paired to it. Use the account that owns the devices, not a guest or shared account.
- **A free Tuya developer account** at [platform.tuya.com](https://platform.tuya.com). This is a separate account from your app login.
- **Reserved IP addresses** for the lights, set in your router's DHCP settings. LocalTuya talks to each device by IP.

A note on networking: LocalTuya's discovery listens for the devices' broadcasts on UDP ports `6666` and `6667`, and control happens over TCP `6668`. Discovery only works when Home Assistant is on the same network and subnet as the lights. If your lights are on a separate VLAN, the broadcasts won't reach Home Assistant on their own, and you'll probably end up adding devices manually, which is more of a pain.

## Step 1: Install HACS and the LocalTuya Fork

I'm not going to go deep on HACS, but here's the short version from the [HACS docs](https://www.hacs.xyz/docs/use/download/download/):

- **Home Assistant OS:** add the `https://github.com/hacs/addons` app repository, then install the **Get HACS** app from **Settings** > **Apps** > **Install app**, start it, and follow the instructions in its log.
- **Container:** run the download script inside the Home Assistant container:

```bash
docker exec -it <home-assistant-container-name> bash
wget -O - https://get.hacs.xyz | bash -
```

Restart Home Assistant, then go to **Settings** > **Devices & services** > **Add integration**, add **HACS**, and complete the GitHub device sign-in.

Now add the fork as a custom repository. When you search HACS for "Local Tuya," you'll see the original listed by default. That's not the one we want.

![HACS search results in the video showing two Local Tuya entries, the default original and the fork updated two days earlier](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/hacs-two-localtuya-entries.jpg)

1. Open **HACS**, click the three-dot menu in the top-right corner, and choose **Custom repositories**.
2. Paste `https://github.com/xZetsubou/hass-localtuya`, set the type to **Integration**, and click **Add**.
3. Open the **Local Tuya** entry from xZetsubou and click **Download**. I just took the latest version.
4. Restart Home Assistant so it loads the integration.

The project's README also has a My Home Assistant button that fills this in for you. The old `xZetsubou/localtuya` URL from my video and notes still redirects on GitHub, but use the current one.

## Step 2: Create a Tuya Cloud Project

While Home Assistant restarts, head over to [platform.tuya.com](https://platform.tuya.com) and create a free account. These steps follow Tuya's current [cloud project guide](https://developer.tuya.com/en/docs/iot/Platform_Configuration_smarthome?id=Kamcgamwoevrx), last updated in August 2026. Tuya changes this portal fairly often, so if a label doesn't match, look for the closest equivalent.

1. Click **Cloud** in the left sidebar, then **Create Cloud Project**.
2. Give it a name. Mine was "Casa."
3. Set **Industry** to **Smart Home** and **Development Method** to **Smart Home**.
4. Pick the **Data Center** that matches your app account's region. I'm in the western US, so I picked Western America. Click **Create**.

![The Tuya Create Cloud Project dialog with the project named Casa and the Industry dropdown open on Smart Home](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/tuya-create-cloud-project.jpg)

Next comes the **Authorize API Services** step. Tuya's guide now says to select **Industry Basic Service**, **Smart Home Basic Service**, and **Device Status Notification**, then click **Authorize**. In the video, the wizard's list looked a little different. I added **Device Log Query** on top of the defaults because I use the device logs later for scenes, and it was already labeled deprecated at the time. If it's still offered, adding it doesn't hurt. If it's gone, use Tuya's current list.

![The Authorize API Services step in the Tuya cloud project wizard as it looked in the video, with the deprecated Device Log Query service being added](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/tuya-authorize-api-services.jpg)

### Link Your Tuya App Account

Now connect the app account that has your lights to this project:

1. In your project, open the **Devices** tab and go to **Link Tuya App Account** > **Add App Account**.
2. Choose **Automatic Link** so every device in the app account comes over.
3. A QR code appears. In the Tuya Smart or Smart Life app, go to the **Me** tab, tap the scanner icon at the top, and scan it.
4. Tap **Confirm** in the app.

Then go to **All Devices** and your lights should show up. If nothing appears, the data center usually doesn't match your app's region. You can change it by editing the project.

In the video, my devices came in with **Read** under **Device Permission**. I clicked **Change** and set each one to **Controllable**. It took a couple tries before they all stuck, so keep refreshing until each device says controllable.

<Warning>
Don't screenshot or share this part of the portal. The project overview shows your Access ID and Access Secret, and the device pages show device IDs and local keys. Anyone with your Access Secret can call Tuya's API against your devices. The QR code links your account too, so keep it to yourself.
</Warning>

### Mind the IoT Core Trial

Tuya's free IoT Core plan is a trial, and when it expires the cloud API stops answering until you renew it. [TinyTuya's setup guide](https://github.com/jasonacox/tinytuya#setup-wizard---getting-local-keys) walks through extending it from the project's **Cloud** page for one, three, or six months at a time. This isn't a disaster for LocalTuya, since it only uses the cloud during setup. Your lights keep working locally either way. You'll just need it again when you add a new device or re-pair one, because re-pairing gives a device a new local key.

## Step 3: Connect LocalTuya to Your Tuya Project

Back in Home Assistant, go to **Settings** > **Devices & services** > **Add integration** and pick **Local Tuya**. Make sure it's the custom one you just installed. The setup page asks for:

| Field | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| **Data Center Region** | The same data center you picked for the project |
| **Client ID** | Your cloud project's **Overview** tab, under the authorization key (Access ID/Client ID) |
| **Client Secret** | The same spot (Access Secret/Client Secret) |
| **User ID** | **Devices** > **Link Tuya App Account**. It's the **UID** next to your app account, not your email. |
| **Username** | Just a name for this hub. I left it as `localtuya`. |

There's also a **Disable Cloud API?** checkbox. You can technically set LocalTuya up without the cloud, but it works a lot better if you at least start with it. The cloud is what fills in local keys and suggests entities for you.

Hit submit and the integration shows up under **Devices & services**. From here on, every change goes through the integration's **Configure** button, which gives you three options: **Add new device**, **Reconfigure existing device**, and **Manage Cloud API account**. That last one is where you'd update the credentials you just entered.

## Step 4: Add the Lights

Before adding devices, close the Tuya and Smart Life apps on your phone. The fork's docs warn that some Tuya devices only accept one local connection at a time.

1. Click **Configure** > **Add new device**.
2. Your discovered devices are listed by name. Pick one and submit. Mine all showed up.
3. On **Configure device connectivity**, the device name, IP address, device ID, local key, and protocol version are filled in for you. My lights showed protocol `3.5`. In most cases you don't need to change anything here.

If a device doesn't show up and the cloud credentials are right, the most common cause is that discovery can't see it, usually because it's on a different subnet. You can pick **Add device manually** from the dropdown and fill in the fields yourself. Manual configuration does work, but it's not great, so get discovery working if you can.

### Finding Device Info Yourself

If you do need to configure manually, or just want to see what a device reports, here's where everything lives on the Tuya platform:

- **Device ID:** your project's **Devices** > **All Devices** list.
- **Local key:** open **Cloud** > **API Explorer**, pick your project, go to the device management APIs, and run **Query Device Details in Bulk** with the device ID. The `local_key` is in the response.
- **Data points (DPs):** in API Explorer, run **Query Properties** under device control with the device ID. Each property has a `dp_id` and a `code`, like `switch_led`, `work_mode`, `bright_value`, `temp_value`, and `colour_data`.
- **Min and max values:** **Devices** > **All Devices** > **Debug Device** > **Device Debugging**, then look at the **Standard Instruction Set**.

API Explorer menu names have moved around over the years, so if you don't see those exact entries, search the API list for "device details" and "properties."

If the integration can't see some DPs during setup, the connectivity page has an optional **Manual DPS** field where you can list them separated by commas, like `20,21,22`.

## Step 5: Configure the Light Entity

After the connection succeeds, LocalTuya asks how to set up the entities. With the cloud configured, **Discover device entities automatically** is worth trying first. I went with **Configure device entities manually** so I could see exactly what was mapped, then chose **Light** as the entity type.

The light form shows every DP the device reports along with its code, which makes this a lot less painful than it sounds.

![The LocalTuya Configure entity form with the DP dropdown open, listing codes like switch_led, work_mode, bright_value, temp_value, colour_data and rgbic_linerlight_scene](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/localtuya-light-dp-list.jpg)

This is how I mapped my 24-bulb acrylic string. Check your own device's DPs, because they can differ between models:

| Field | What I picked |
|---|---|
| **ID** | `20` (`switch_led`) |
| **Friendly name** | Same as the device name in the app |
| **Brightness** | `22` (`bright_value`) |
| **Color Temperature** | `23` (`temp_value`) |
| **Brightness Lower / Upper Value** | `10` / `1000` |
| **Color Mode aka Work Mode** | `21` (`work_mode`) |
| **Color** | `24` (`colour_data`) |
| **Minimum / Maximum Color Temperature in K** | Left at the defaults, `2700` / `6500` |
| **Scene** | `56` (`rgbic_linerlight_scene`) |
| **Music mode available?** | Off |

The brightness range is the one to watch. The form defaulted the lower value to `29`, but the device's real range was different. To check, go to **Debug Device** > **Device Debugging** on the Tuya platform and look at the **Standard Instruction Set**. On my lights, `bright_value` runs from `10` to `1000` and `temp_value` from `0` to `1000`, so I set the brightness range to match.

![The Tuya Device Debugging page showing the Standard Instruction Set with bright_value ranging from 10 to 1000 and temp_value from 0 to 1000](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/tuya-standard-instruction-set.jpg)

Leave **Scene values** empty for now. I wanted to make sure the basics worked first. I also left music mode off because I haven't tested it and probably won't use it.

Submit, keep **Finish configuring entities** checked, and you're done. I opened the device in Home Assistant, flipped it off and on, picked a color, and slid the color temperature warmer. All of it worked with just this basic configuration, which was a fantastic sign after the night I'd had.

## Adding the App's Scenes

The scenes took me a while to figure out. The scene DP takes a big raw string that describes the whole animation, and I don't know how to decode it into something human-friendly. So for now, the scenes come from the app.

I've collected the scene strings for my 24-bulb acrylic lights in the [LocalTuya section of my homelab repo](https://github.com/TechHutTV/homelab/tree/main/homeassistant/localtuya). Each line is the raw value, a colon, and the name you want in Home Assistant:

```text
ASMJMgkAAABkAABkAAAAAGQAPGQAAAAAZAC7VAAAAABkAOpXAAAAAGQBCGMAAAAA: Christmas
AVATMhkAAABkALxkAAAAAGQALU4AAAAAZAAAZAAAAABkAGQ8AAAAAA==: Christmas eve
ASkJMk8AAABkAN1LAAAAAGQAvVMAAAAAZADYXQAAAAA: Fathers Day
AQEDMjIAAABkAUVkAAAAAGQBIjkAAAAAZAEeZAAAAABkATpfAAAAAGQAAWQAAAAAZAAnWgAAAAA=: Flower harbor
ASoJMgoAAABkAAFkAAAAAGQAiGQAAAAAZAC7ZAAAAAA: Football Day
AScJMiQAAABkAJVbAAAAAGQAtGQAAAAAZACRYQAAAAA: Forest Day
ASUKMjIAAABkAApfAAAAAGQBBmQAAAAAZADvXAAAAABkALAsAAAAAGQAfFQAAAAA: Halloween
ASgKMgcAAABkAU49AAAAAGQBF1UAAAAAZAEMOwAAAAA: Mothers Day
ASYUMjIAAABkADxVAAAAAGQBD2QAAAAAZADNQwAAAABkABZgAAAAAA: Thanksgiving
ASQMMjIAAABkARJkAAAAAGQA71cAAAAAZAFBUwAAAABkAVJkAAAAAA: Valentines
```

These are scene definitions, not keys, so they're safe to share. They were captured from my specific lights, though, so on a different model they might not look right. Capturing your own (next section) is the reliable way.

To add them:

1. Open the LocalTuya integration and click **Configure** > **Reconfigure existing device**.
2. Pick the light and submit. Leave the connectivity settings as they are and submit again.
3. On the light entity, paste the lines into **(Optional) Scene values**, one scene per line.
4. Submit and finish.

Now the light has an **Effect** dropdown in Home Assistant with every scene you added. I flipped through Halloween, Flower Harbor, and Forest Day, and they all played exactly like they do in the app.

![The XMcosy Acrylic (24) light in Home Assistant with the effect list open, showing Christmas, Christmas eve, Fathers Day, Flower harbor and Football Day](/docs-static/img/2026/10/localtuya-home-assistant-holiday-lights/home-assistant-effect-scenes.jpg)

## Capturing Your Own Scenes

Copying my scenes is cool, but you'll probably want ones I don't have, or your own DIY designs. The trick is to activate the scene in the app and then read what the device reported in Tuya's device logs.

1. In the Tuya app, activate the scene you want. If it's already active, switch to a different one first and then back, so a fresh event gets logged.
2. On the Tuya platform, go to **Devices** > **All Devices** > **Debug Device** for that light, and open **Device Logs**.
3. Look for the newest **Colorful scene** event. The event details hold the raw string. My notes also mention filtering the logs to scene events with the dropdown at the top and clicking **Search**. If the newest event doesn't appear, refresh the whole page.
4. Copy the string, go back to **Reconfigure existing device** in LocalTuya, and add a new line under **Scene values** in the form `string: Name`.

I did this with a premade one from the app's mood section called Planet Journey, which switches between blue and purple. After adding it, I jumped between Halloween and Planet Journey in Home Assistant and it was exactly right.

Your own designs work the same way. I made a DIY scene in the app that alternates red, white, and blue, saved it as a July 4th scene, activated it, grabbed the string from the logs, and added it as `July 4`. It showed up in the Effect list and worked perfectly.

If you want to dig into what these strings actually contain, they're Base64. My notes link Tuya's [lighting instruction set docs](https://developer.tuya.com/en/docs/iot/dj?id=K9i5ql3v98hn3) as a starting point for decoding them.

## Final Thoughts

Overall, I'm very happy with these lights, even though it took me way too long to get here. It seems so simple once it's done, but between different protocol versions and guides that are out of date, making it simple was the hard part. The key was the fork. If you have newer Tuya lights that refuse to work with the original LocalTuya, try xZetsubou's version before anything else.

There's still stuff I'd love to have. If I could figure out how to decode that raw scene string, I think these could all happen with some scripting:

- Per-bulb control with a custom card
- Saving the current look as a scene directly in Home Assistant
- Effect modes like pulse and fade in Home Assistant

If you know more about this type of RGB data than I do, come help out on my [GitHub](https://github.com/TechHutTV/homelab/tree/main/homeassistant/localtuya). I'll keep adding to those notes as I figure more out. With all that, I do hope you have an absolutely beautiful day.
